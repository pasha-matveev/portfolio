## The game

Two teams of small autonomous robots play soccer in the Open League: no remote control, everything is decided on board. A robot has to find the ball, know where it is on the field, avoid the opponents, and score, all in real time.

## My part

I spent years in the Moscow Robotics Club, building several versions of robots in the Lightweight and then Open Soccer categories. The team ranged from three to five people. In 2025 I was the lead programmer; before that I led the team, and at times I was also responsible for the PCB and 3D design.

## What's inside the robot

What follows combines my own work on localization and strategy with the team's current design.

<Gallery>
  <Figure src="/projects/robocup-soccer/exploded.jpg" kind="Photo" ratio="4 / 5" caption="Exploded view: LiDAR and mirror camera on top, electronics, kicker and power in the middle, drive at the bottom." />
  <Figure src="/projects/robocup-soccer/optics.png" kind="Screenshot" caption="Optics design: ray diagram of the camera looking up into the conical mirror." />
</Gallery>

**Localization from a LiDAR in C++.** An RPLidar delivers 5,000 samples per second. I wrote a custom version of the Minimum Bounding Rectangle algorithm to find the field walls in the point cloud, which gives the robot's exact position; the clusters left inside the field are other robots. A gyroscope tells which side of the field the robot is facing.

**Vision.** A camera with a 220° lens looks up into a custom mirror that was calculated, 3D-printed, sanded and chrome-plated, so the whole field fits into one frame. It runs at 50 fps on the Raspberry Pi with no neural networks: a colour threshold finds the ball, a minimum enclosing circle fits it, and an `atan2` gives its angle and distance.

**Path planning.** I wrote a simulator that tests strategy without the robot, and implemented Artificial Potential Fields for obstacle avoidance, plus Dubins paths so the striker reaches the ball already facing the goal. A spin shot turns the robot 115° through the dribbler and curves the ball behind the keeper.

<Gallery>
  <Figure src="/projects/robocup-soccer/dubins.png" kind="Screenshot" caption="Dubins path: the striker reaches the ball already facing the goal." />
  <Figure src="/projects/robocup-soccer/spin-shot.png" kind="Screenshot" caption="Spin shot: a fast turn through the dribbler curves the ball behind the keeper." />

**Drive.** Three omnidirectional wheels at 120° let the robot move in any direction while turning, at up to 2 m/s. Brushless motors with 16-bit encoders talk over CAN, and an MPU-6050 gyroscope holds the heading.

**Dribbler and kicker.** The dribbler rollers are cast in our own 3D-printed moulds and sit on a floating mount with two degrees of freedom; a light gate tells whether the robot has the ball. The kicker is a solenoid charged to 48 V, and the pulse length sets the power from a soft pass to a full shot at up to 5 m/s.

**Electronics.** A Raspberry Pi 4 runs vision, positioning and strategy, while an ATmega328 handles the drive, dribbler and kicker over UART. The whole robot runs on four 18650 cells.
